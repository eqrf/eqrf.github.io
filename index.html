Mission 3: How RSV Builds a "Syncytium" Monster

Pathology Goal: Analyze how RSV alters normal cell and tissue architecture.

🦠 Why is it called "Syncytial"? RSV carries a special weapon on its surface called the F-Protein (Fusion Protein). Once RSV infects an epithelial cell, it forces the cell membranes of neighboring cells to melt and fuse together! Multiple cells merge their cytoplasm into one giant, dying blob with multiple nuclei called a syncytium.

Mission 4: The Histology & Microscopy Lab

Histology Goal: Prepare a tissue slide using fixation and H&E staining, then diagnose the patient.

🧪 Why do we stain tissues? Real cells are mostly clear! To see them under a microscope, histologists preserve the tissue (Fixation) and dip it in two dyes called H&E (Hematoxylin & Eosin):
  • Hematoxylin (Purple/Blue): Stains the Nuclei deep purple.
  • Eosin (Pink/Red): Stains the Cytoplasm and proteins bright pink.
